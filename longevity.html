Longevity

"The goal is not to live forever. The goal is to live long, well, and useful — and to compress the years of weakness at the end."

Philosophy

Adding healthy years. Not just years.

Longevity is not a wish. It's a daily discipline of small decisions compounded across decades — what you eat, how you move, how you sleep, what you put into your body, and what you protect it from.

My framework is informed by five of the most rigorous voices in modern longevity. I do not follow any single one of them dogmatically. I take the highest-conviction principles from each, apply them as an athlete and operator living in the UAE, and refine through experience and data.

These are the protocols I practice. They are not medical advice. They are a framework.

The Protocols

What I practice daily.

Organised around the inputs that move the needle most — sleep, light, food, water, movement, recovery, and mind.

01

Morning Light & First Hour

How you start the day sets the circadian clock for everything that follows.

Sources: Huberman · Brecka
  • 5–10 minutes of direct sunlight within 30 minutes of waking. No sunglasses, no window glass — outside, eyes open.
  • Hydrate before food: water + electrolytes + a pinch of sea salt.
  • Delay caffeine by 90–120 minutes after waking — allows adenosine to clear naturally.
  • Breathwork before screens — 3–5 minutes of nasal breathing or physiological sighs.
02

Sleep

The single most underrated longevity intervention.

Sources: Attia · Huberman · Johnson
  • Cool bedroom — 18–19°C (65°F). The body needs to drop core temperature to sleep deeply.
  • Complete darkness. Blackout shades. Cover or remove all LEDs.
  • Consistent sleep and wake times — including weekends.
  • Seven to nine hours, non-negotiable. Sleep debt cannot be repaid with caffeine.
  • No screens or overhead lights in the 90 minutes before sleep.
  • Last meal three hours before bed where possible.
03

Food

Eat real food. Mostly animals. Don't drink your calories.

Sources: Saladino · Attia · Brecka
  • Animal-based foundation — grass-fed beef, lamb, eggs, raw or full-fat dairy.
  • Organ meats weekly — liver is the most nutrient-dense food on earth.
  • Eliminate seed oils completely — canola, soybean, sunflower, corn, vegetable, "vegetable" blends.
  • Eliminate ultra-processed food. If it has a barcode and an ingredients list longer than three lines, it is not food.
  • Carbohydrates from real sources — raw honey, ripe fruit, raw dairy, white rice for training days.
  • Protein priority — aim for 1g per pound of target bodyweight.
04

Hydration & Electrolytes

Most people are chronically dehydrated and electrolyte-depleted. In the UAE, the deficit is severe.

Sources: Brecka · Huberman · Attia
  • Spring or properly filtered water as the foundation. Not tap. Not plastic-bottled when avoidable.
  • Hydrogen water daily — a Brecka protocol I've adopted for its anti-inflammatory and cellular signalling effects.
  • Electrolytes every morning — approximately 1,000 mg sodium, 200 mg potassium, 60 mg magnesium.
  • More on training days. Sweat loss in Abu Dhabi heat is significant and often underestimated.
  • Avoid commercial sports drinks loaded with sugar and food colouring.
05

Blue Light & Circadian Discipline

Light is the most powerful signal you give your body. Master light, master your biology.

Sources: Huberman · Attia
  • Bright sunlight in the morning — see Protocol 01.
  • No overhead lights after sunset. Lamps only, low and warm.
  • Blue-light blocking glasses after dark when working on screens.
  • Phones on red-light or Night Shift filter after 19:00.
  • No screens in bed — period.
  • Mid-day sun exposure when possible — at least 10 minutes of bright light supports daytime alertness and night-time sleep.
06

Training

Strength is the most important variable for longevity after 40. Cardiovascular fitness is a close second.

Sources: Attia · Johnson
  • Resistance training 3–4 times per week — compound lifts, full-body coverage.
  • Zone 2 cardio 3 times per week, 45–60 minutes. Conversational pace, nasal breathing.
  • VO₂ max intervals once per week — the 4×4 protocol (4 min hard, 4 min recovery, repeat).
  • Daily steps — at least 8,000–10,000.
  • Stability and mobility woven through the week — protect joints, especially as load increases.
  • One full rest day per week — recovery is when adaptation happens.
07

Recovery — Cold, Heat, Breath

Stress the system. Then let it adapt.

Sources: Brecka · Huberman
  • Cold exposure — 11 minutes per week total, split across multiple short sessions.
  • Sauna — 4 sessions per week, 20 minutes each at 80°C. Strongest evidence base for cardiovascular and mortality benefit.
  • Breathwork daily — physiological sigh, box breathing, or Wim Hof depending on the need.
  • Active recovery — long walks, easy swims, mobility work between hard training days.
08

Supplementation

Targeted. Evidence-based. Bloodwork-driven.

Sources: All five · individual stack varies based on testing
  • Creatine monohydrate — 5g daily. The most researched supplement in sports science.
  • Omega-3 (EPA + DHA) — 2–3g daily, from high-quality fish oil.
  • Vitamin D3 + K2 — dosed by blood level, not by label.
  • Magnesium glycinate — evenings, supports sleep and recovery.
  • Methylated B vitamins (Brecka protocol) — particularly relevant for MTHFR variants.
  • Trace minerals and electrolytes — daily, baseline.
09

Mind & Stress

You cannot outrun a stressed nervous system.

Sources: Huberman · Brecka
  • NSDR (Non-Sleep Deep Rest) — 10–20 minutes when energy dips, mid-afternoon ideal.
  • Daily breathwork — see Protocol 07.
  • Time in nature without a phone — minimum once per week.
  • Reading before sleep — analog books, not screens.
  • One day per week with reduced inputs — fewer meetings, fewer notifications, more presence.
